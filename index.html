William J. Kezerian

Creole linguistics · Syntax · Computational linguistics

I am a PhD student in the Department of Linguistics at the University of Pennsylvania. My research spans computational linguistics, French-based Creoles, language contact, syntax, and experimental methods. I'm interested in uses of NLP for documentation, bilingual education, and collaborative experimental linguistics.

I recently completed my MA in Linguistic Studies at Syracuse University, where I was advised by Dr. Jaklin Kornfilt. My thesis used NLP methods to compare elicitations and natural usage of focus and complementation structures in Guadeloupean Creole, using data collected during NSF-sponsored fieldwork.

Research

  • Variation in French-based Creoles How speakers vary in their use of grammatical markers, currently focusing on complementizer kè in Guadeloupean Creole, comparing experimental judgments with spontaneous speech.
  • Syntax of complementation and focus Clause boundaries, fronting, and focus constructions in Guadeloupean Creole, and what they reveal about Creole clause structure in comparative perspective.
  • Computational methods for under-resourced languages Using NLP to support language documentation, corpus analysis of fieldwork data, and native-language education in Creole-speaking communities.
