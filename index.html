William J. Kezerian

Creole linguistics · Syntax · Computational linguistics

I am a PhD student in the Department of Linguistics at the University of Pennsylvania, advised by Marlyse Baptista, and am affiliated with the Language Contact and Cognition Lab and the Syntax Lab. My research spans computational linguistics, French-based Creoles, language contact, syntax, and experimental methods. I'm interested in uses of NLP for documentation, bilingual education, and collaborative experimental linguistics.

I recently completed my MA in Linguistic Studies at Syracuse University. My thesis used NLP methods to compare elicitations and natural usage of focus and complementation structures in Guadeloupean Creole, using data collected during NSF-sponsored fieldwork.

Research

  • Variation in French-based Creoles How speakers vary in their use of grammatical markers, currently focusing on complementizer kè in Guadeloupean Creole, comparing experimental judgments with spontaneous speech.
  • Syntax of complementation and focus Clause boundaries, fronting, and focus constructions in Guadeloupean Creole, and what they reveal about Creole clause structure in comparative perspective.
  • Computational methods for under-resourced languages Using NLP to support language documentation, corpus analysis of fieldwork data, and native-language education in Creole-speaking communities.
  • Form-Function Convergence in LLM Language Acquisition Adapting Baptista, Gelman & Beck’s (2016) convergence hypothesis to a language model, using frozen embedding manipulation to make L1–L2 form–function alignment a controlled variable. Convergent morphemes are acquired faster in both production and comprehension, but the effect reverses when the borrowed L1 form is an ambiguous cue to its function. Collaborators: Marlyse Baptista, Lyle Ungar, Margaret Kandel, and Yourdanis Sedarous.
